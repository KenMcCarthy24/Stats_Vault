
Statistical Inference is the process of using data from a sample to make "educated guesses" or decisions about a larger population. Its difficult to measure every single member of a population, so inference allows us to quantify how much we can trust our sample results.

Core concepts:
* **The Population:** The entire group we care about (represented by fixed, usually unknown **parameters** like $\mu$ or $\sigma$).
* **The Sample:** The subset we actually measure data for (yielding **statistics** like $\bar{x}$ or $s$).
* **The Inference:** The logical step of using the statistic to represent the parameter, while accounting for the "noise" or error inherent in sampling.

Most inference will fall into one of two categories:
* **Estimation**: What is the [[Estimators|most likely value]] of this population characteristic given the sample and how [[Confidence Intervals|confident]] are we about that?
* **Hypothesis Testing**: Is this claim about the population supported by the data?
